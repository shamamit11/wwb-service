# SEO Strategy: Wide Web Blog

## Document Purpose

This document defines the long-term SEO strategy for Wide Web Blog. It is intended to guide both developers and content creators in building a technical publication that earns authority, trust, and sustainable organic traffic in a focused set of technical domains.

This strategy assumes:

- human-reviewed technical publishing
- strong topical concentration
- cluster-based content architecture
- platform-level support for structured SEO workflows
- AI-assisted operations without mass autonomous content generation

## SEO Philosophy

Wide Web Blog should treat SEO as discoverability architecture for valuable technical content, not as a traffic extraction game.

The site should compete by being:

- narrower than generic AI sites
- deeper than broad software blogs
- more structured than hobby publications
- more trustworthy than mass AI-generated content operations

The goal is to build a durable authority site where search growth comes from:

- strong topical coverage
- editorial consistency
- internal content architecture
- technical accuracy
- author credibility
- platform-level SEO discipline

## SEO Vision

## Long-Term SEO Goals

- become a recognized authority domain in AI Agents, MCP, Laravel + AI, AWS + Automation, Software Architecture, and Developer Productivity
- build a search engine footprint dominated by evergreen, high-intent technical content
- create an internal content graph where pillar pages and supporting content reinforce one another over time
- establish a site that can scale SEO quality through systems, not shortcuts

## Authority-Building Strategy

Wide Web Blog should build authority through depth, repetition of quality, and clear domain boundaries.

The authority strategy should be:

- focus tightly on a defined set of overlapping technical domains
- publish dense clusters before expanding into adjacent areas
- maintain updateable pillar pages as canonical topic hubs
- strengthen author trust and editorial consistency
- create implementation-aware content that demonstrates genuine expertise

Authority should be earned at three levels:

- page level: individual articles satisfy specific search intent
- cluster level: groups of related articles reinforce expertise
- domain level: the site becomes associated with the target technical themes

## Internal Linking Strategy

Internal linking should be treated as an editorial requirement and a platform capability.

### Core Rules

- every article must link to its primary pillar
- every pillar must link to all major supporting articles in its cluster
- supporting articles should cross-link horizontally to related content
- no article should remain orphaned
- update cycles should include internal link improvement as a formal task

### Link Types

- upward links: supporting article to pillar page
- downward links: pillar page to supporting articles
- lateral links: related supporting articles within or across clusters
- pathway links: beginner article to intermediate or advanced content

### Minimum Linking Standard

- 3 internal links minimum for short articles
- 5 internal links minimum for standard evergreen articles
- 8+ internal links for pillar pages and flagship guides where relevant

### Example Linking Pattern

An article on `How AI Agent Memory Works` should link to:

- the main AI Agents pillar
- the Retrieval, Context, and Memory pillar
- an AI architecture or observability article

## Content Standards

Every published article should meet a minimum SEO and editorial standard before publication.

## Minimum Publishing Requirements

### Word Count

- short support article: 1,000 to 1,400 words minimum
- standard evergreen article: 1,500 to 2,500 words
- flagship article or pillar page: 3,000+ words

Word count is a floor, not the objective. Pages should be as long as needed to satisfy intent completely.

### Internal Links

- minimum 3 to 5 internal links depending on article type
- at least one link to a primary pillar
- at least one link to a closely related supporting article when available

### External References

- at least 2 credible external references for factual or standards-based content when applicable
- use primary sources where possible: official documentation, specifications, original project docs, or vendor sources

### Images

- at least 1 purposeful image, diagram, or visual aid for most articles
- pillar pages and tutorial content should use diagrams or architecture visuals where useful
- every image must have descriptive alt text

### FAQs

- include an FAQ section when the query intent clearly includes follow-up questions
- use FAQ sections selectively, not mechanically

### Schema

- `Article` schema on all published articles
- `FAQPage` schema only when FAQ content is real and useful
- `BreadcrumbList` schema on indexable content pages

### Readability

- short paragraphs
- clear H2 and H3 structure
- accurate terminology
- concrete examples over vague abstraction
- avoid filler sections added only for SEO length

## Technical SEO

Technical SEO should be implemented as a product capability, not left to manual publishing habits.

## URL Structure

Wide Web Blog should keep URLs simple, stable, and human-readable.

### Recommended URL Patterns

- homepage: `/`
- blog index: `/blog/`
- category archive: `/category/{slug}/`
- article detail: `/{slug}/` or `/blog/{slug}/`
- author page: `/author/amit-kumar-sharma/`
- pillar hub when needed: `/guides/{slug}/`

### Recommendation

Prefer one consistent article pattern and do not over-nest URLs. Simplicity is better than deep taxonomy in the URL.

## Slug Strategy

- slugs should be concise, readable, and durable
- avoid dates in slugs
- avoid version numbers unless the page is explicitly version-specific
- avoid unnecessary stop words when clarity is preserved
- allow manual override when editorially justified

### Slug Examples

- `/what-are-ai-agents/`
- `/mcp-server-architecture/`
- `/laravel-ai-queue-patterns/`
- `/aws-automation-small-teams/`

## Canonical URLs

- every indexable page must output a self-referencing canonical by default
- canonical overrides should be allowed only when there is a clear duplication or consolidation need
- do not canonicalize substantially different articles to a pillar page
- paginated pages should canonicalize to themselves unless a specific strategy is chosen

## Meta Tags

Every indexable article should support:

- custom `title`
- custom `meta description`
- default fallbacks when omitted
- robots directives when needed

### Metadata Standards

- title target: 50 to 60 characters when practical
- description target: 140 to 160 characters when practical
- titles should prioritize clarity and specificity over clickbait

## Open Graph

Every article should support:

- `og:title`
- `og:description`
- `og:type`
- `og:url`
- `og:image`
- `og:site_name`

Recommendation:

- use a dedicated Open Graph image when possible for flagship content
- fall back to featured image when no dedicated social image exists

## Twitter Cards

Support:

- `summary_large_image` for most article pages
- `twitter:title`
- `twitter:description`
- `twitter:image`

## Sitemap

The sitemap should be generated automatically from published content only.

### Include

- published articles
- pillar pages
- category pages if indexable
- author pages if indexable and valuable

### Exclude

- drafts
- thin utility pages
- admin pages
- search result pages
- staging or internal pages

### Recommendation

- regenerate or refresh the sitemap on post publish, unpublish, or significant URL changes

## RSS

Provide a clean RSS feed for:

- latest articles
- optionally category-specific feeds later

RSS supports:

- syndication
- recurring readership
- distribution tooling

## Robots.txt

The robots policy should be conservative and explicit.

### Allow

- public article pages
- category pages intended for indexing
- author pages intended for indexing

### Disallow

- `/admin/`
- internal preview paths
- draft or staging routes
- parameter-heavy utility routes if introduced later

Robots rules must not be used as a substitute for correct noindex behavior where that is needed.

## Structured Data

Structured data should be consistently generated through the platform, not manually pasted into pages.

## Article Schema

Use `Article` or `BlogPosting` for published articles.

### Include

- headline
- description
- image
- datePublished
- dateModified
- author
- publisher
- mainEntityOfPage

### Implementation Recommendation

- generate from `posts`, `seo_metadata`, `users`, and `media`
- only output for published content

## FAQ Schema

Use only when the page contains a real FAQ section with meaningful questions and answers.

### Implementation Recommendation

- build from structured FAQ blocks if the content model supports them
- do not auto-generate FAQ schema from unrelated headings

## Breadcrumb Schema

Use `BreadcrumbList` for all indexable articles and category pages.

### Example Path

- Home
- Category
- Article

### Implementation Recommendation

- generate from category assignment and route structure
- keep breadcrumb output aligned with on-page navigation

## Organization Schema

Use `Organization` schema for Wide Web Blog as the publisher entity.

### Include

- name
- URL
- logo
- sameAs profiles when available

### Implementation Recommendation

- render sitewide in the layout or homepage context

## Website Schema

Use `WebSite` schema to represent the publication and support site-level identity.

### Include

- site name
- URL
- publisher relationship
- search action only if on-site search is introduced and technically implemented

## Structured Data Implementation Recommendations

- generate schema server-side through the SEO module
- centralize schema builders by page type
- validate markup as part of publishing QA
- store only configurable inputs in the database; derive the final payload in code

## SEO Scoring System

The platform should support a future internal SEO score to guide editors before publish.

The score should assist decision-making, not override editorial judgment.

## Scoring Dimensions

Suggested total score: `100 points`

### 1. Content Quality: 30 points

- primary query intent is clearly satisfied: 10
- article depth fits its type and topic: 10
- structure and headings are coherent: 10

### 2. Metadata: 15 points

- custom title present and strong: 5
- meta description present and useful: 5
- canonical and robots settings valid: 5

### 3. Internal Links: 15 points

- links to primary pillar: 5
- links to relevant supporting content: 5
- no obvious orphaning or missed cluster links: 5

### 4. External References: 10 points

- relevant credible references included: 5
- references improve trust or factual grounding: 5

### 5. Images And Media: 10 points

- featured or supporting image present where appropriate: 4
- alt text complete: 3
- image improves understanding: 3

### 6. Schema And Technical SEO: 10 points

- required schema valid: 4
- metadata fields map correctly into markup: 3
- page meets technical SEO requirements: 3

### 7. Readability And UX: 10 points

- heading structure is clear: 3
- paragraph and section flow is readable: 3
- formatting supports scanability: 4

## Suggested Score Interpretation

- `90-100`: strong publish-ready asset
- `75-89`: acceptable but should improve if time allows
- `60-74`: publish only with explicit editorial reason
- `below 60`: not ready

## Scoring Methodology

- combine automated checks with editorial inputs
- automated checks should evaluate measurable criteria
- editorial review should score content quality and intent satisfaction
- preserve explanations for low subscores so the system is actionable
