# Content Strategy: Wide Web Blog

## Document Purpose

This document defines the editorial and SEO content strategy for Wide Web Blog. Its purpose is to establish how the publication will build topical authority, compound organic search growth, and publish consistently high-trust technical content across a focused set of domains.

## Strategic Position

Wide Web Blog is not designed to compete as a generic AI content site. It should compete as a technical publication with:

- narrow topic focus
- strong editorial judgment
- implementation-aware content
- cluster-based SEO architecture
- AI-assisted workflows that increase velocity without lowering trust

The content strategy must therefore optimize for authority density, not sheer content volume.

## Core Content Focus

Wide Web Blog will focus on six core areas:

- AI Agents
- MCP
- Laravel + AI
- AWS + Automation
- Software Architecture
- Developer Productivity

These are not six disconnected categories. They should be treated as overlapping thematic systems that reinforce each other:

- AI Agents connects to MCP, automation, and architecture
- Laravel + AI connects to agents, architecture, and developer workflows
- AWS + Automation connects to deployment, orchestration, and agentic systems
- Software Architecture connects to every pillar through system design and implementation tradeoffs
- Developer Productivity connects to tooling, workflows, and applied automation

## Strategic Goals

- build topical authority in a concentrated technical niche
- produce evergreen search assets with long shelf life
- create a content library that compounds through internal linking
- establish pillar pages that organize and distribute authority
- publish enough supporting content to turn each pillar into a defensible cluster
- use AI as workflow leverage, not as an autonomous publishing engine

## Pillar Page Strategy

Pillar pages should serve as:

- canonical topic introductions
- navigational hubs for supporting articles
- editorial standards for each topic area
- internal linking anchors
- long-term update targets

Each pillar page should:

- define the topic clearly
- explain why it matters
- map key subtopics
- link out to supporting cluster content
- be updated as the cluster matures

Pillar pages should not try to rank for every variation on day one. Their role is to establish semantic breadth and provide an authoritative entry point that strengthens over time.

## Supporting Content Strategy

Supporting content should exist to deepen each pillar. It should not be treated as disconnected post inventory.

Supporting content types should include:

- beginner-to-intermediate explainers
- implementation tutorials
- architecture breakdowns
- comparisons
- troubleshooting guides
- workflows and playbooks
- opinionated best-practice pieces
- glossary or terminology explainers where useful

### Content Mix Recommendation

Across the first year, aim for:

- 20% pillar and flagship content
- 45% supporting cluster tutorials and explainers
- 20% architecture, workflow, and implementation breakdowns
- 10% comparison and decision content
- 5% timely commentary only when it strengthens authority

Timely content should be limited. The site should bias toward evergreen and updateable assets.

## Internal Linking Strategy

Internal linking should be intentional and systematized.

### Principles

- every supporting article should link to its parent pillar
- every pillar should link to all major cluster articles
- related cluster articles should link laterally to each other
- comparison posts should link to both compared concepts and their parent pillar
- glossary or foundational explainers should link upward into pillars and downward into implementation posts

### Linking Model

Use a hub-and-spoke pattern:

- pillar page as hub
- supporting articles as spokes
- cross-links between high-relevance spokes

### Minimum Linking Rules

- every new article should include at least 3 internal links to existing relevant content
- every updated pillar page should add links to newly published supporting content
- every article should be assigned one primary pillar and up to two secondary cluster relationships

### Link Intent Types

- conceptual links: for foundational understanding
- implementation links: for practical execution
- comparison links: for evaluation and decisions
- progression links: for guiding readers deeper into a topic

## Publishing Frequency

The first year should prioritize consistency and quality over aggressive volume.

### Recommended Cadence

- Months 1-3: 2 articles per week
- Months 4-6: 2 to 3 articles per week
- Months 7-12: 3 articles per week if editorial quality remains strong

### Annual Target

- 110 to 130 published articles in year one

This gives enough capacity to:

- launch 12 pillar pages
- publish at least 80 cluster-supporting assets
- reserve some bandwidth for updates, comparisons, and tactical opportunities

## Content Lifecycle

Content should be managed as an asset lifecycle, not a one-time publish event.

### Lifecycle Stages

1. Idea
2. Approved Topic
3. Briefed
4. Drafting
5. Review
6. SEO Review
7. Publish Ready
8. Published
9. Update Candidate
10. Refreshed or Consolidated

### Lifecycle Rules

- ideas do not enter drafting until approved against a pillar or cluster
- AI-generated content remains draft until human review
- published content should be reviewed periodically for freshness and internal link coverage
- underperforming overlapping articles should be merged or repositioned

## Topic Approval Workflow

Topic approval should be strict enough to preserve focus.

### Approval Criteria

A topic should be approved only if it:

- clearly maps to a core pillar
- serves a real developer or builder intent
- deepens cluster authority rather than repeating existing coverage
- fits the publication’s technical depth standard
- can be supported by credible examples or implementation insight

### Approval Questions

- Which pillar does this belong to?
- What reader problem does it solve?
- Is it evergreen, updateable, or strategically timely?
- Does it strengthen a cluster gap?
- Can we say something better or more specific than existing generic coverage?

### Approval Outcomes

- approved for pillar development
- approved as supporting cluster content
- deferred for later
- rejected as off-strategy

## Knowledge Base Integration

The knowledge base should act as the editorial context engine behind the content strategy.

### Knowledge Base Uses

- store pillar definitions and editorial framing
- store recurring terminology and canonical definitions
- capture internal linking targets by topic
- track article angles already covered
- store quality standards for tutorials, comparisons, and architecture pieces
- maintain reusable research notes and references

### Operational Role

Before drafting, each article should pull from the knowledge base for:

- approved terminology
- related content references
- product or domain-specific framing
- reusable explanations that need consistency

This reduces inconsistency across articles and strengthens cluster coherence.

## AI-Assisted Content Workflow

AI should assist the editorial process, not replace it.

### AI-Suitable Tasks

- topic expansion from approved pillar areas
- outline generation
- brief drafting
- structural rewrite suggestions
- metadata suggestions
- internal link suggestions
- title variation generation
- content refresh analysis

### AI-Restricted Tasks

- final publication without review
- unsupported technical claims
- original opinion presented without editorial validation
- large-scale autonomous posting

### Suggested Workflow

1. Human approves topic against a pillar.
2. AI helps expand the topic into angle options and an outline.
3. Human selects structure and adds domain-specific guidance.
4. AI may help produce a working draft or section suggestions.
5. Human rewrites, validates, improves examples, and removes generic output.
6. AI may suggest metadata and internal links.
7. Human performs final technical and editorial review.
8. Article is published only after human approval.

## Publishing Program Structure

For the first 12 months, each month should generally include:

- 1 pillar or flagship article
- 4 cluster-supporting evergreen articles
- 1 implementation or comparison piece
- optional 1 refresh or update if cadence allows

This creates a consistent structure without making the roadmap too rigid.

## Content Refresh Strategy

Topical authority is built not only by publishing new pieces but by strengthening existing ones.

### Refresh Cadence

- monthly: update 1 to 2 high-potential articles
- quarterly: review all pillars for internal link completeness and factual freshness
- biannually: consolidate overlapping thin articles

### Refresh Triggers

- ranking decline
- new product or protocol changes
- missing internal links
- outdated examples
- stronger cluster support available

## Success Metrics

The content strategy should be judged by authority and compounding outcomes, not only raw publishing counts.

### Editorial Metrics

- articles published per month
- percentage of posts mapped to a pillar and cluster
- time from approved topic to published article
- percentage of posts updated within the refresh cycle

### SEO Metrics

- number of ranking keywords across target clusters
- organic clicks to pillar pages
- cluster-level internal link density
- share of traffic going to evergreen assets
- growth in branded and topic-specific search impressions

### Authority Metrics

- number of cluster-supporting articles per pillar
- ratio of pillars with at least 6 strong supporting assets
- backlinks or citations to flagship content
- returning readership to core topic areas

## Summary

Wide Web Blog should build its content strategy around a dense pillar-and-cluster model in a narrow technical niche. The first year should prioritize authoritative evergreen content, disciplined internal linking, and consistent publishing across AI Agents, MCP, Laravel + AI, AWS + Automation, Software Architecture, and Developer Productivity. AI should accelerate research, structuring, and optimization, but human judgment should remain the mechanism that protects trust and turns the content library into a durable SEO asset.
